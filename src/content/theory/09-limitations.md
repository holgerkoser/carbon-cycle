## Limitations

The models are deliberately simple. For the question of how much CO₂ remains in the atmosphere when the reserves are burned, these simplifications matter most:

- **No climate–carbon feedbacks:** the solubility of CO₂ does not decrease in a warmer ocean, soil respiration does not increase with temperature, and permafrost, fires and forest dieback are missing. All of them would leave more CO₂ in the atmosphere, so the concentrations until 2300 tend to be a lower bound. Full Earth system models disagree strongly on the size of these feedbacks, mainly on land.
- **CO₂ fertilization:** in both models the land sink rests on the logarithmic fertilization with a single factor $\beta$. Nutrient limitation could weaken it in the future; it is the largest uncertainty of the land sink.
- **Ocean transport:** Tomizuka’s deep ocean exchanges carbon about ten times faster than radiocarbon measurements indicate, so it removes CO₂ too quickly after 2100. The Bern ocean is calibrated with radiocarbon, but its land biosphere has no regrowth after land-use change and needs a strong fertilization to match today’s land sink.
- **No carbonate dissolution in sediments:** the neutralization of CO₂ by ocean sediments over thousands of years is not modelled. It plays a minor role until 2300.
- **Temperature:** the energy balance model has a single global feedback and no state dependence; other forcings are approximated as a fixed share of the CO₂ forcing.
- **Reserves are not a fixed quantity:** they change with prices, technology and exploration. The reserve values should refer to the end of 2024; values from older statistics overestimate today’s reserves by the production since then.
- **Lignite:** the split of historical coal emissions (7.5 % lignite) and the lignite share of today’s coal emissions in the projection (about 4 %) are assumptions.
- **Observations:** the Global Carbon Budget’s land sink and its ocean sink before 1959 are themselves model estimates. The calibration is therefore partly a comparison with other models.
- **Concentration conversion:** $\varepsilon$ is computed from the total mass of the atmosphere. The Global Carbon Budget uses 2.124 PgC/ppm for the dry-air mole fraction; the difference is about 0.5 %.
