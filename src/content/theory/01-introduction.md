## Introduction

A box model of the carbon cycle is used to show how atmospheric CO₂ has developed over the past 250 years and how carbon flows between the individual reservoirs today. Driven by projected emissions, the model also yields future levels of atmospheric CO₂ and the resulting warming.

The central question of this application is: **how does burning today’s proven fossil fuel _reserves_ affect the CO₂ concentration of the atmosphere?** Reserves are the deposits that are known and economically recoverable today. They are much smaller than the _resources_, which include deposits that are not yet recoverable or not yet proven.

The starting point is

> Tomizuka, Akira. [Is a box model effective for understanding the carbon cycle?](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.3013196) American Journal of Physics 77.2 (2009): 156–163.

Two carbon cycle models run side by side with the same emissions:

- the **seven-box model of Tomizuka**, which describes the ocean, the biosphere and the soil by well-mixed boxes;
- the **Bern model** used by the IPCC, whose ocean transport is calibrated with radiocarbon and which describes the ocean and the land biosphere by pulse response functions.

Tomizuka’s model reproduces the historical record more closely; the Bern model is more plausible on time scales of centuries. Where they disagree, the difference shows how uncertain the long-term answer is.

All parameters can be changed in the sidebar of the dashboard. The model is solved again on every change.
